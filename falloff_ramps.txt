# Light Falloff Ramps Research Report

This report outlines modern and classic techniques for controlling point and spot light distance falloff in lightmap baking. The goal is to provide mappers with alternatives to the raw 1/d² law that prevent mathematical singularities (hotspots) and offer better artistic control.

## 1. Inverse-Square with Minimum Size (Offset Model)
This is the most common modern approach (used by Frostbite, Unity HDRP, and Bakery). Instead of a point singularity, it treats the light as having a physical volume.

**Formula:**
Intensity = Photons / (dist² + size²)

**Parameters:**
- `size`: The virtual radius of the light (e.g., 0.1 to 10.0 units).

**Characteristics:**
- **Prevents Hotspots:** The peak intensity at dist=0 is capped at `Photons / size²`.
- **Physicality:** At distances much larger than `size`, it behaves exactly like the 1/d² inverse-square law.
- **Usage:** Ideal for realistic scenes where lights are placed very close to walls or ceilings.

---

## 2. Constant-Linear-Quadratic (CLQ Model)
The classic approach from the Source Engine and older Quake-based toolsets. It gives artists direct control over the attenuation curve.

**Formula:**
Intensity = Photons / (C + L*dist + Q*dist²)

**Parameters:**
- `C` (Constant): Controls the peak intensity at the source.
- `L` (Linear): Provides a slower, more even fade.
- `Q` (Quadratic): The physical inverse-square term.

**Characteristics:**
- **High Control:** Setting L=1 and Q=0 creates a linear falloff. Setting C=1, L=0, Q=1 mimics a softened inverse-square.
- **Non-Physical:** Allows for "unrealistic" but visually pleasing lighting that travels further than physical light would.
- **Usage:** Great for "fill lights" or stylised environments.

---

## 3. Epic/Karis "Windowed" Falloff
The model used in Unreal Engine 4/5. It combines a softened inverse-square law with a windowing function to ensure the light fades to exactly zero at a specific radius.

**Formula:**
Attenuation = [ 1 / (dist² + 1) ] * [ clamp(1 - (dist / Reach)⁴, 0, 1) ]²

**Parameters:**
- `Reach`: The maximum distance the light can travel (already exists as "reach" in most compilers).

**Characteristics:**
- **No Hard Cuts:** Standard inverse-square light often "cuts off" abruptly when it hits the maximum reach distance. This formula forces a smooth fade-to-zero at the boundary.
- **Leak Prevention:** Because it hits zero exactly, it prevents "light leaking" through thin geometry at long distances.
- **Usage:** Best for large scenes where performance (culling lights by radius) and visual consistency are both important.

---

## 4. Rational / Smoothstep Falloff
A purely artistic, non-physical ramp that prioritizes smoothness over realism.

**Formula:**
Intensity = Photons * [ 1 / (1 + (dist / Reach)²) ]

**Characteristics:**
- **No Hotspots:** The curve is extremely flat near the source.
- **Slow Decay:** It maintains energy much better than inverse-square.
- **Usage:** Perfect for "glow" effects or ambient light sources where you want a very soft, broad pool of light.

---

## Comparison Summary

| Model | Complexity | Realism | Hotspot Prevention | Best For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Offset** | Low | High | Excellent (via Size) | Realistic PBR scenes |
| **CLQ** | High | Low-Medium | Good (via Constant) | Artistic/Legacy control |
| **Windowed** | Medium | High | Good (+1 offset) | Avoiding hard radius cuts |
| **Rational** | Low | Low | Perfect | Soft glow / Ambient fill |
