# Status Report: Atomic Unit Trisoup Merging (Phase 1)

## What We Are Doing
We are overhauling the BSP geometry pipeline to enhance visual quality by introducing rounded corners (chamfers) at the intersection of structural brushes. 
The immediate goal (Phase 1) is to seamlessly merge the generated "chamfer strips" with their originating planar BSP fragments into a single `MST_TRIANGLE_SOUP` mesh. This involves:
1. Identifying the parent planar fragments that generated the strips.
2. Triangulating the parent fragment and the strips.
3. Welding their vertices to form a unified, seamless mesh.
4. Repacking their UV lightmap coordinates using XAtlas.

## What We Did
1. **Implemented `MergeChamferStripsIntoParents`**:
   - Loop over the base `mapDrawSurface_t` fragments.
   - Triangulate the parent n-gon into a CCW triangle fan.
   - Locate any chamfer strips generated by `ChamferSurfaceEdges` that reference this parent (`parentSurfaceNum`).
   - Triangulate the chamfer strips and weld their vertices to the parent.
   - Convert the resulting unified mesh into a `miscModel` (atomic trisoup).

2. **Fixed XAtlas Indexing Bug**:
   - We discovered that `GenerateAtomicUVsWithXAtlas` was silently discarding our newly generated chamfer triangles if XAtlas managed to pack the UVs without physically splitting any vertices (`xm->vertexCount == ds->numVerts`). 
   - We corrected this by forcing the vertex and index arrays to *always* be rebuilt from the XAtlas output, ensuring the triangles match what the engine renders.

3. **Attempted Multi-Surface Coplanar Merging (Early Phase 2)**:
   - We observed that converting corner fragments to Trisoups caused the engine to stop dynamically merging them with adjacent planar wall fragments (resulting in visibly "split" walls in `r_showTris`).
   - In an attempt to solve this immediately, we bypassed the `!hasStrips` check to convert *all* wall fragments into Trisoups, and wrote `MergeCoplanarTrisoups` to flood-fill and merge them.
   - While it successfully merged many surfaces, it incorrectly merged surfaces that were not parented and possibly non-coplanar, introducing new complexities.

## Where We Are Going
Based on the current state, we are taking a step back to strictly enforce the boundaries of Phase 1:
1. **Restore Strict Parenting Check**: We must re-introduce the `if (!hasStrips) continue;` check so that ONLY fragments that actually generated chamfer strips are converted to Trisoups.
2. **Refine Coplanar Merging**: The flood-fill algorithm in `MergeCoplanarTrisoups` merged surfaces it shouldn't have. We will defer full-wall merging and first perfect the atomic unit merging (Parent + Strips only) to ensure the core geometry is flawless before scaling up.

## Original Implementation Plan (Phase 1)

**Goal**: Implement a targeted geometry modification step that processes subdivided planar faces and their associated chamfer strips, merging them into a single, seamless `MST_TRIANGLE_SOUP` per fragment.

### `q3map/qbsp.h`
- Add `qboolean planarDerived;` to `mapDrawSurface_t`.

### `q3map/tjunction.c`
- Inject `MergeChamferStripsIntoParents` to loop over all fragments.
- Promote the fragment to an `MST_TRIANGLE_SOUP` (`miscModel = qtrue`).
- Repack the UVs for the resulting triangle soup using XAtlas.

### `q3map/bsp.c`
- Call `MergeChamferStripsIntoParents` after `ChamferSurfaceEdges`.

## Corrections Made After the Implementation Plan
1. **XAtlas Array Rebuilding**: Changed the UV generation to unconditionally copy `xm->indexArray` back to `ds->indexes`, because XAtlas always rearranges the vertex order even if the vertex count remains the same.
2. **Dynamic Chamfer Width Scaling**: Ensured `chamferwidth` dynamically scales down (maintaining a 10x ratio) for extremely thin BSP slices to prevent overlapping geometry, which was rendering the chamfer strips microscopic and invisible on thin fragments.
3. **Introduced MergeCoplanarTrisoups**: Added a post-processing step to stitch adjacent Trisoups back together to bypass the Quake 3 lightmap limitations (`enforceSampleSize` and 512x512 page limits), though this needs refinement to restrict its aggressive merging.
